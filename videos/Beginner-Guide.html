First, get comfortable

Video 0:08

Start in Practice

  1. Open Bridge → Practice on this iPhone. Practice sends nothing to Xbox.
  2. Touch a button and watch it highlight. Drag a stick and watch its position change.
  3. Tap Stop when you want every phone action released. In some settings this is called Release all.
Center

Stops sticks and actions generated by their movement. Separate button holds can remain on.

Stop / Release all

Clears phone holds, repeats, timed actions and floating sessions. It keeps your bridge connection enabled.

Stop affects this app. It does not stop a separately connected wheelchair joystick.

Choose an action without typing

In Edit, hold a button and open Button actions. Select one action, or several to press together. Custom payload is the advanced text option; turning it on replaces these checkbox choices. You can return to the saved choices by turning Custom payload off.

The one-finger favorite

Video 0:18

Walk, run and jump with one finger

Stick+ keeps your stick movement and adds actions as your finger travels farther from the center. A threshold is simply the distance that starts an extra action.

  1. Tap Edit, then hold the walking stick.
  2. Choose Input mode → Stick+. Choose a direction, such as Up.
  3. Set the actions for Normal, Boost and Super boost. Adjust the two distances.
  4. Tap Done → Save. Try a small, medium and farther movement in Practice.
Small moveWalkFartherAdd sprintOuter stageAdd jump

The built-in Apply forward example uses left-stick click above 50% and A above 95%. Those buttons only mean sprint and jump when your game uses that mapping. Change them for your game.

Need repeated presses? Enable Repeat Stick+ buttons and choose its interval. The stick keeps moving while its chosen buttons repeat. Move back to an empty stage, use Center, or use Stop to end it.

Keeping sprint during a jump, and other tuning

Each Stick+ stage replaces the previous one. To keep sprint while adding jump, include sprint in both stages. The stage sends its own actions; it does not borrow another visible button’s Hold Toggle settings.

Sensitivity and response change the movement feel. Extended Boost adds farther-out distances or another stage. Leave enough reachable space for those movements. Directional / WASD sends assigned actions instead of analog stick movement; keyboard actions can also reach the free Windows or Mac receiver. They are not sent through the Pico console bridge.

With Auto-center off, a retained stage can keep repeating after you lift. To change centering during play, add an Auto-center toggle control. It temporarily switches sticks and stick-style Button Aim controls without rewriting their saved settings. Turning centering on stops movement retained after lift. Standard stick tuning offers sensitivity and response; the separate Button Aim settings have additional distance and deadzone options.

Touch, drag, act

Video 0:42

Aim and fire together

Button Aim means “send a button action while dragging a stick.” It does not need finger-contact calibration.

  1. In Edit, add a Button, or hold one you already have.
  2. Under Button actions, choose LT for aiming, RT for firing, or both. Use the actions your game needs.
  3. Turn on Button Aim. Open Aim movement settings → Aim output → Right stick.
  4. Choose When touched to start immediately. Adjust sensitivity, then Done → Save.

Now touch and drag the pad to aim while its actions are active. For aim-only, choose LT without RT. For an ordinary fire-only button, choose RT and leave Button Aim off.

Keeping fire on, firing on lift, and Alternate

With When touched, Button Aim starts immediately. Add Hold to toggle if you want its action to stay held after the hold time; tap again to release. This differs from an ordinary Hold Toggle button, which waits before its first action.

When released sends the chosen action on an intentional lift. Boundary options can cancel it when the finger leaves the button. Alternate action changes later touches to a second action phase; its reset hold returns to the base action. Stop resets the phases.

Aim can start from the button center or where your finger first lands. You can adjust sensitivity, response, vertical inversion, movement distance and centering. Cancelled touches do not fire a release action.

Combine or separate

Video 0:58

Drive and steer with one touch

  1. Add a button and choose the game’s accelerator action, commonly RT.
  2. Turn on Button Aim, with Aim output → Left stick.
  3. Choose a comfortable shape and sensitivity. Save and test it in Practice.

Dragging steers while the button holds acceleration. A separate brake button can use LT. A steering-only pad uses left-stick Button Aim with no button action selected.

A circular control can send up/down as well as left/right for a game that uses those directions to fly. A wider rectangular pad may feel comfortable for side-to-side steering; its shape alone does not disable vertical output.

Stop walking, then pick something up

On an ordinary interaction button, enable Center left stick before pressing. It clears walking before sending that button, while keeping right-stick aim and other button holds available.

USB buttons trigger mode gives full acceleration or no acceleration. Partial throttle requires the matching adjustable-output hardware.

Important differences between the choices

An ordinary Hold Toggle button does nothing before its hold timer is reached. A button with Double-tap to repeat can still send normal single taps, and can use Hold Toggle for a steady hold instead. Auto-Tap is a different mode and replaces Hold Toggle.

Profile options → Tap ordinary buttons to hold makes simple buttons latch immediately on a tap. Specialized controls retain their own behavior. The centering shortcut can turn Auto-center on or off across that profile’s sticks and Button Aim controls. TouchAim always centers on lift.

Leaving the app clears live actions. A saved layout remembers the settings, not an old held or repeating input.

Small controls, useful shortcuts

Video 1:34

More actions from one small button

A small D-pad

Add a button with no ordinary action selected. Enable Button Aim → Aim output → D-pad. Choose four directions, or eight including diagonals. Set a comfortable activation distance. Save, then drag from that button in each direction.

A weapon wheel

Choose your game’s wheel-opening button, enable Button Aim → Right stick, and use When touched. Hold and drag to choose a direction. Check the actual game’s mapping—there is no universal wheel button.

Slide between buttons

Open Edit → Profile options and choose Slide between buttons or Slide between controls. Ordinary buttons also need Allow activation by sliding enabled.

Directional actions and direct-touch controls

The separate Directional button option adds your chosen actions at two drag distances in each direction. Its second stage adds both directional actions; Stick+ instead replaces one stage with another.

Button Aim, directional buttons, TouchAim and certain special controls require direct touch and do not accept slide-in activation. Page changes and local actions also have their own touch rules. Sliding is not a blanket setting for every kind of control.

Meet your finger where it lands

Video 1:48

Bring a control to your finger

Floating controls appear near a touch inside an activation area. You can use a single button, a group, or a ring. Their original layout remains available for restoring later.

  1. In Edit, open Add → Floating controls.
  2. Choose a button/group, or create a ring. Set a reachable activation area and comfortable target sizes.
  3. Save the layout. Touch the activation area to bring it up.
  4. Use Finish to close that floating session. Stop clears all phone sessions.

Rings use distance, not finger size. Drag among their movement, aiming and boost areas to combine actions. This is a useful alternative when TouchAim cannot distinguish comfortable contacts.

What a ring can do

Rings can have movement, one to three aiming knobs, fine aim, and bands that tap, hold or repeat. Movement and aim can have separate actions and distances. You can keep selected actions after lift, use intentional-release firing, or choose direct boost touches.

Finish can require a direct tap. Other floating-control buttons can clear or center only a chosen session. A group can appear without pressing a member, and can allow sliding between its members. If a whole arrangement will not fit, reduce its size or adjust its activation area.

Comfort comes first

Video 2:00

TouchAim: contact size, not pressure

Button Aim combines a drag with chosen actions. TouchAim additionally reads the finger’s contact measurement to choose between stages, such as aim and shoot.

  1. Open the TouchAim control’s settings, then Open touch inspector.
  2. Compare two comfortable ways of resting your finger while moving. Contact radius estimates contact size; it does not measure pressure.
  3. If the readings are clearly different, use manual thresholds or Calibrate Aim and Shoot, then complete its validation.
  4. If comfortable contacts look alike, choose Button Aim or a ring instead. There is no need to press harder.
On iPhone 15

Force is unavailable. A larger contact reading is not a force reading. The app cannot reliably tell two identical contact readings apart.

Stages, calibration and imported settings

TouchAim offers manual two-state, manual three-stage, and calibrated modes. Stages can press or hold actions and have different aiming sensitivity or confirmation times. A lower return threshold reduces accidental switching near a boundary.

Use this calibration appears only after a successful check. Imported sensor actions stay off until reviewed on the current phone or freshly calibrated. TouchAim centers aiming on lift. A configured Hold Release Delay can keep an action briefly afterward; Stop cancels pending output.

A combo without staying pressed

Video 2:18

One tap can finish a sequence

Enable Macro Mode in a button’s behavior. One tap starts its sequence; lifting your finger does not cut it short. Further taps during the run are ignored. Stop cancels it.

Each command automatically releases after 200 milliseconds unless you give it a different duration. Multiple untimed actions start together. A sequence needs different start delays.

Optional recipe: tap A, then B
  1. Enable Custom payload and enter X360A,X360B(D400).
  2. Enable Macro Mode, then Done → Save.
  3. Tap once in Practice. A presses for 200 ms. B starts 400 ms after the tap and presses for 200 ms.

This is an example of timing, not a recommended game combo. Every D delay counts from the initial tap; commas do not add waiting time.

Adjusting lengths and other advanced timing

U100 gives an action a 100 ms press from the time that action starts. For example, X360A(U100),X360B(D400)(U100) makes the same sequence with shorter presses. Give repeated presses of the same action a gap between their held periods.

Ordinary mode also supports actions on release, repeats, trigger Pulse, WAIT and Hold Release Delay. Their rules differ: an ordinary delayed action usually ends when you lift; Macro Mode finishes its finite run. Macro Mode ignores global Hold and Turbo, and does not use indefinite R repetition.

Advanced payloads can also hold a fixed stick direction and amount from a button. Conflicting stick outputs follow ownership rules so another command cannot simply take over an occupied stick.

Arrange once; play comfortably

Video 2:28

Make the whole screen work for you

  1. Tap Edit. Drag controls to comfortable spots, including the edges.
  2. Hold a control to change its name, shape, size or actions. Use Add for a new control.
  3. Tap Done to return to the draft, then Save to keep it. Cancel returns to the saved layout.

Portrait and landscape have their own arrangements. Display settings choose light/dark appearance and your preferred play orientation. Screen corners and the phone’s cutout can still cover pixels, so check visibility where you place controls.

Top menu

Tap Hide for a small menu circle. Drag the circle; tap it to reopen the top menu.

Actions toolbar

Drag its arrow handle. Use … for edge positions or Vertical toolbar. The minus button gives it a separate movable circle.

Menu and toolbar positions save automatically, separately from profiles. Moving them releases current actions without requiring a manual bridge reconnect. Reset position restores positions without erasing your controls.

Your controller, organized

Video 2:50

Keep games and navigation together

A profile is a complete controller. A page is one screen inside it, such as Walk, Drive or Menus.

  1. Open Profiles → Game starters.
  2. Choose Shooter, Racing, Sports or Open World. The app adds your own editable copy.
  3. Use Page to find its other controls. The starter’s Menus page has D-pad, A, B, Menu and View.

Changing profiles or pages releases old actions while keeping bridge connection intent. You do not need to press Connect again. Each game may use different button assignments.

Menu and View are supported Xbox outputs. The Xbox-logo/Guide and Share actions are available in Practice but are not sent through this bridge.

Learn locally; keep what works

Video 3:02

Explore extra tools and keep a backup

Desktop tools

Touchpads can show pointer movement, clicks, click locks and scrolling. Buttons can produce keyboard and mouse actions. Camera Follow uses left-stick travel to generate gentle horizontal right-stick turning; your manual right-stick touch takes priority.

Use the free Windows or Mac receiver for these desktop tools and Camera Follow. On Windows, install the receiver software and documented virtual-controller driver. On Mac, use Start-Mac-Receiver.command and follow its Python and Accessibility instructions. Enter the computer’s local IP address and port 42734 in the app. Mac maps existing gamepad controls to keyboard/mouse and does not provide analog gamepad output. No Pico or extra hardware is needed for PC play. These desktop outputs are not available through the Pico console bridge. Camera Follow does not use the phone’s camera.

Touchpad choices

Move only does not click. Hold while touching holds left-click until lift. Tap to lock / unlock locks it after a short delay, then another tap unlocks. Tap click / double-tap lock supports a quick click or a double-tap-and-hold lock. A separate Scroll toggle changes movement into scrolling. Stop clears locks and returns to pointer mode.

Save what feels good

  1. Use a profile’s options to Copy profile before experimenting.
  2. Use Manage reusable settings to keep named stick, Button Aim, TouchAim or floating-control presets.
  3. Use Export all profiles for a backup. Import iPhone profiles or preview an Android conversion later.

Recovery documents offers copies saved before certain changes. Android originals remain available unchanged even when some settings cannot be converted. Backups do not include bridge connection details or live held input.

Saving a reusable preset is separate from saving a layout. Cancel layout does not remove a preset you already saved.

Match the software to your setup

Video 3:16

Connect when you are ready

The iPhone needs a configured bridge and matching hardware to control Xbox. The app does not pair directly to Xbox as a Bluetooth controller.

  1. Open Bridge → Bridge settings. Use the address, port, protocol and trigger mode for your installed Pico firmware.
  2. Use Save and connect. Wait until the bridge is ready before touching controls.
  3. Test movement, buttons and releases before playing. Confirm the actual game’s button mapping.
Trigger modeWhat it means
USB buttons (full press)Any positive LT/RT amount becomes a full press. Zero releases. Xbox Accessories must map the intended USB inputs to LT/RT.
Adjustable (circuit)Partial trigger amounts require the matching analog circuit and firmware.
USB adjustable (Cronus Zen)Requires a Zen and matching Pico firmware. This route remains experimental until physically verified with the intended setup.

Practice can display a saved 50% trigger value even when USB buttons sends 100% to Xbox. Changing an app setting does not install different firmware on the Pico.

Reopening, Disconnect and Practice

After valid settings are saved, bridge mode reconnects when you reopen the app. It always starts with released controls—old holds and macros do not resume. Page changes, profile changes and Stop keep connection intent; a brief released-control check can pause input.

Disconnect stops attempts for the current visit. After actually leaving and reopening the app, the saved bridge can reconnect. Choose Practice on this iPhone to stay disconnected across app openings. Unsaved form edits are not used, and invalid saved settings need correction.

Leaving the app releases its actions immediately. If the bridge is unavailable, the open app keeps inputs released while retrying. This does not control the independent chair Pico.
